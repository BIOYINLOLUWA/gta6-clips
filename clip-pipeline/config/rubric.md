# What makes a GTA 6 stream clip work

Candidate windows come from spikes in chat speed and loudness. A spike says something happened.
Decide whether it is a clip, and exactly where it starts and ends.

1. The first two seconds explain why to keep watching. Start right before the setup (a cop car appearing,
   "watch this", a plan being announced), not two topics earlier.
2. There is a payoff and it lands inside the clip: the crash, the escape, the fail, the line that made chat
   laugh. End 1-2 seconds after the payoff, on the reaction, not 20 seconds later.
3. It stands alone. Someone who never saw the stream understands it. Skip jokes that need context.
4. The streamer reacts. A loud reaction to a normal moment beats quiet impressive gameplay.
5. No dead air: silence, menus, loading, map or inventory screens. Skip or cut around them.
6. Short beats long: 20-45 seconds by default, never over 59.

Best GTA 6 moment types: police chases and escapes, crashes and ragdoll fails, heists going wrong, absurd NPC
behaviour, perfect stunts, the streamer losing it, funny exchanges with chat or a co-streamer.

Do not pick: isolated story cutscenes, in-game TV/radio/comedy shows on their own, story reveals or the ending,
cheats/exploits/mod menus, moments carried by copyrighted music, private info or chat harassment, sponsor reads,
stream settings talk, graphic violence in the first 3 seconds.

Scoring 1-10: 9-10 viral on its own, 7-8 strong, 6 fine, under 6 drop. Minus 2 if it needs context,
minus 2 if the payoff is weak or missing. Fewer strong clips beat many weak ones.
